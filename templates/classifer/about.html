Technical Overview[cite: 2]

From satellite scan to fire classification

Four machine learning models fused into one system that reads thermal radiation from orbit and decides, in under a second, whether what is burning below is a wildfire or an industrial heat source[cite: 2].

The satellite data

Every 12 hours, the VIIRS sensor aboard Suomi-NPP and NOAA-20 sweeps every point on Earth at 375 metre resolution[cite: 2]. Two thermal bands capture the scene[cite: 2]:

BandWavelengthWhat it sees
I-4 (M13)3.55 to 3.93 microns[cite: 2]Peak emission from actively burning material at 600 to 1200 K[cite: 2]
I-510.5 to 12.4 microns[cite: 2]Ambient land surface temperature, the thermal background[cite: 2]

When a pixel is significantly hotter than its neighbours, the FIRMS algorithm flags it as a thermal anomaly and records brightness temperature, Fire Radiative Power (FRP) in megawatts, scan geometry, and acquisition time[cite: 2]. This project uses 200,000 such records covering South Asia from 2012 to 2023, with 97.8% matched to real satellite swath files[cite: 2].

The full pipeline

01
Sensor[cite: 2]
VIIRS I-band radiance capture[cite: 2]
Raw at-sensor radiance from VNP02IMG, geolocated by VNP03IMG at 375 m per pixel[cite: 2]
02
Detection[cite: 2]
FIRMS contextual fire algorithm[cite: 2]
Compares each pixel's I-4 brightness to a background window, flags anomalies, computes FRP from Stefan-Boltzmann law[cite: 2]
03
Labelling[cite: 2]
Proximity to Global Power Plant Database[cite: 2]
Haversine distance to 35,000 real plants[cite: 2]. Within 5 km means Industrial[cite: 2]. Otherwise Wildfire[cite: 2]. Zero manual annotation needed[cite: 2].
04
Patches[cite: 2]
64 by 64 pixel thermal image extraction[cite: 2]
Each detection becomes a 64x64 crop from the VNP02IMG swath, centred on its pixel, normalised to 0 to 1 range[cite: 2]
05
Experiment 1[cite: 2]
PCA: spatial compression and reconstruction error[cite: 2]
Flatten 64x64 to 4096 dimensions, compress to 50 principal components[cite: 2]. Reconstruction error measures how anomalous the shape is[cite: 2]. Wildfire patches are irregular and compress poorly[cite: 2]; industrial hotspots are compact and compress cleanly[cite: 2].
06
Experiment 2[cite: 2]
CNN: learned spatial pattern recognition[cite: 2]
Three convolutional blocks extract blob size, edge sharpness, symmetry, and spread into a 128-dimensional feature vector[cite: 2]. Trained end-to-end on the 64x64 patches[cite: 2].
07
Experiment 3[cite: 2]
FIRMS tabular: spectral and temporal features[cite: 2]
Random Forest on brightness, FRP, I4 minus I5 contrast, FRP per pixel area, day/night flag, cyclic hour and month encoding[cite: 2]. Industrial sources appear in both day and night passes; wildfires are mostly daytime[cite: 2].
08
Experiment 4[cite: 2]
Hybrid fusion: 193-dimensional late fusion[cite: 2]
Concatenate PCA features (51-d), CNN features (128-d), and FIRMS tabular features (14-d) into a single vector[cite: 2]. LogisticRegression trained on the combined representation achieves the highest AUC of all four experiments[cite: 2].
