Technical Overview

From satellite scan to fire classification

Four machine learning models fused into one system that reads thermal radiation from orbit and decides, in under a second, whether what is burning below is a wildfire or an industrial heat source.

The satellite data

Every 12 hours, the VIIRS sensor aboard Suomi-NPP and NOAA-20 sweeps every point on Earth at 375 metre resolution. Two thermal bands capture the scene:

BandWavelengthWhat it sees
I-4 (M13)3.55 to 3.93 micronsPeak emission from actively burning material at 600 to 1200 K
I-510.5 to 12.4 micronsAmbient land surface temperature, the thermal background

When a pixel is significantly hotter than its neighbours, the FIRMS algorithm flags it as a thermal anomaly and records brightness temperature, Fire Radiative Power (FRP) in megawatts, scan geometry, and acquisition time. This project uses 200,000 such records covering South Asia from 2012 to 2023, with 97.8% matched to real satellite swath files.

The full pipeline

01
Sensor
VIIRS I-band radiance capture
Raw at-sensor radiance from VNP02IMG, geolocated by VNP03IMG at 375 m per pixel
02
Detection
FIRMS contextual fire algorithm
Compares each pixel's I-4 brightness to a background window, flags anomalies, computes FRP from Stefan-Boltzmann law
03
Labelling
Proximity to Global Power Plant Database
Haversine distance to 35,000 real plants. Within 5 km means Industrial. Otherwise Wildfire. Zero manual annotation needed.
04
Patches
64 by 64 pixel thermal image extraction
Each detection becomes a 64x64 crop from the VNP02IMG swath, centred on its pixel, normalised to 0 to 1 range
05
Experiment 1
PCA: spatial compression and reconstruction error
Flatten 64x64 to 4096 dimensions, compress to 50 principal components. Reconstruction error measures how anomalous the shape is. Wildfire patches are irregular and compress poorly; industrial hotspots are compact and compress cleanly.
06
Experiment 2
CNN: learned spatial pattern recognition
Three convolutional blocks extract blob size, edge sharpness, symmetry, and spread into a 128-dimensional feature vector. Trained end-to-end on the 64x64 patches.
07
Experiment 3
FIRMS tabular: spectral and temporal features
Random Forest on brightness, FRP, I4 minus I5 contrast, FRP per pixel area, day/night flag, cyclic hour and month encoding. Industrial sources appear in both day and night passes; wildfires are mostly daytime.
08
Experiment 4
Hybrid fusion: 193-dimensional late fusion
Concatenate PCA features (51-d), CNN features (128-d), and FIRMS tabular features (14-d) into a single vector. LogisticRegression trained on the combined representation achieves the highest AUC of all four experiments.
